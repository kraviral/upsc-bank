# GS3 — Economy, Agriculture, Environment, Science & Technology, Security

Indian economy and planning · inclusive growth · government budgeting · land reforms · investment models · infrastructure · agriculture, MSP, PDS, food processing, marketing · environment, conservation, pollution, EIA, disaster management · science and technology, indigenisation, IPR · internal security, cyber security, money laundering, border management.

## What GS3 actually rewards

GS3 is the paper where **specificity is the differentiator**. Everyone can say "improve infrastructure". The marks go to the answer that names the constraint, the mechanism, and who bears the cost. Every recommendation must be traceable to a cause the answer has already established — a solutions list that would fit any GS3 question earns nothing.

## Framework library

### Core lenses

| Purpose | Lens |
|---|---|
| Any policy question | **Short term → medium term → long term** |
| Any commodity or sector | **Value chain**: production → processing → storage → transport → marketing → consumption |
| Diagnosing a shortfall | Demand-side vs supply-side constraints |
| Who is involved | Centre · State · private sector · farmer/consumer · regulator |
| Weighing an intervention | Cost–benefit **with the distributional question attached**: who gains, who bears it |
| Broad policy sweep | PESTLE — political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental |
| Structural view | Primary · secondary · tertiary, or formal vs informal |
| The permanent GS3 trade-off | Growth vs equity vs sustainability |
| Reform sequencing | Legal → institutional → financial → technological → behavioural |

### Environment

- **DPSIR** — Driver → Pressure → State → Impact → Response
- Mitigation vs adaptation; polluter pays; precautionary principle; common but differentiated responsibilities
- Ecosystem services framing — what the resource does for free before it is destroyed
- Conservation vs livelihood vs development as a three-way claim on the same land

### Disaster management

Prevention → mitigation → preparedness → response → recovery. Hazard × vulnerability ÷ capacity. Structural vs non-structural measures.

### Security

- **Threat → vulnerability → capability → response**
- Actors: state · non-state · proxy · lone actor
- Domains: land · sea · air · cyber · space · information
- Kinetic vs non-kinetic; grey-zone and hybrid warfare
- Root causes → sustaining factors → triggers, for insurgency and extremism

### Science & technology

Research → development → deployment → diffusion. Dual-use dimension. Access, affordability, and the regulatory lag behind every new technology.

## Value addition

- **Economic Survey and NITI Aayog** findings; **Budget** provisions; **Acts** by name.
- **Data**: attach a figure only if you are sure of it. Otherwise state the *direction* of change and mark `[verify]`. A confidently wrong number does more damage than a qualitative claim.
- **Schemes** with the mechanism, not the acronym expansion.
- **Keywords**: crowding out · twin balance sheet · JAM trinity · formalisation · import substitution vs export orientation · terms of trade · monsoon dependence · fragmentation of holdings · circular economy · just transition · carbon intensity vs absolute emissions · dual-use technology · critical minerals · supply-chain resilience · grey-zone warfare · radicalisation.

## Intro types that suit GS3

A data point that frames the scale of the problem · a recent policy decision or event · the structural paradox (record production alongside farmer distress) · a scheme's stated aim against its outcome · a comparative reference to another economy.

## Conclusion types that suit GS3

A named, specific reform · phased roadmap · SDG linkage · the sustainability constraint · an institutional fix rather than a call for awareness.

## Diagram

Value-chain flow · virtuous or vicious cycle · 2×2 trade-off matrix · before-and-after comparison · DPSIR loop · layered security architecture · a simple supply-and-demand cross where it genuinely clarifies.

## Recurring GS3 traps

- Generic solutions ("increase investment", "raise awareness") detached from the diagnosis.
- Quoting a statistic with false precision. If unsure, give the direction and flag it.
- Environment answers that ignore the livelihood side, or livelihood answers that ignore the ecological limit.
- Security answers that list agencies instead of analysing the threat.
- Treating a scheme's launch as evidence of its outcome.
- Ignoring the state-government role in a subject that is on the State or Concurrent List.
